Logits are the raw, unnormalized prediction scores generated by the very last layer of an LLM before they are converted into actual probabilities.
Think of logits as the model's raw voting tallies for every single word in its vocabulary. If a vocabulary contains 50,000 possible tokens, the model will output a list of 50,000 positive or negative numbers. A higher logit score means the model strongly believes that specific token should be the next word in the sentence.

The Logits Pipeline in a Transformer :

[Transformer Hidden Layers] 
          │
          ▼
   [Linear Layer]         ───► Outputs Logits (Raw scores: e.g., "apple": 12.4, "the": -2.1)
          │
          ▼
  [Softmax Function]      ───► Converts to Probabilities (Percentages summing to 100%)
          │
          ▼
 [Decoding Algorithm]     ───► Selects the next token (Greedy vs. Sampling methods)

Key Attributes of Logits:

1. Mathematical Form: Logits are continuous real numbers ranging from negative infinity to positive infinity (-∞ to +∞). They do not add up to 1 or 100%.
2. The Softmax Bridge: Because raw numbers are hard to sample from, logits are passed through a mathematical function called Softmax. This function squashes the numbers into a range between 0 and 1, turning them into clean percentages that add up to exactly 1.0 (100%).
3. Where Hyperparameters Step In: When you adjust generation settings like Temperature, you are physically altering the logits before they hit the Softmax layer. Dividing the logits by a low temperature pushes the highest logit even higher (making the model more confident/greedy), while a high temperature flattens the differences between logits (making the model more random).
